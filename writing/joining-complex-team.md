## Experience of Joining a New Team, Project, and Complex Environment

When I joined the Macy’s team at HashedIn, I had to absorb a significant amount of information in a short period of time, including the business domain, system architecture, development processes, integration patterns, and team responsibilities. One of the biggest challenges was gathering and understanding extensive business requirements while navigating frequently evolving priorities and changing expectations.

To build clarity, I first focused on the most valuable sources of information, including project documentation, architecture diagrams, code repositories, issue trackers, and discussions with experienced team members. I created a structured understanding of the system by mapping key components, data flows, dependencies, and areas that required further exploration. Rather than attempting to learn everything at once, I prioritized the modules most relevant to my immediate responsibilities and business goals.

As I became more familiar with the project, I actively engaged with stakeholders to clarify requirements, identify pain points and bottlenecks, and document expectations in a structured manner. I regularly evaluated multiple implementation approaches, discussing trade-offs with the team to ensure that technical decisions aligned with evolving business needs. This helped us adapt quickly to changing requirements while minimizing rework and maintaining project momentum.

To accelerate my learning, I took ownership of small, well-defined tasks that allowed me to gain hands-on experience with the codebase and system workflows. I asked focused questions, validated assumptions early, and continuously updated my notes and understanding based on feedback. Over time, this approach enabled me to build context quickly, reduce ambiguity, and become an effective contributor while delivering solutions that balanced business requirements, technical feasibility, and long-term maintainability.
